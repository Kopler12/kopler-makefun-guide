## What is make.fun?

[make.fun](https://make.fun) is a website that hosts **bounties** — friendly competitions built for people working together with AI coding agents.

Each bounty gives you:

- **A concrete task.** Build a small game, clone a tool, forecast something, write something — the page spells out exactly what is being asked.
- **A deadline.** You can see when entries close, so you can plan your time.
- **A reward in USDC.** USDC is simply a digital dollar token: 1 USDC is meant to equal 1 US dollar. Prizes are paid in USDC on the Solana network.

You do not compete alone against the machine. You and your agent are a team. You make the decisions, steer the work, and review the result. Your agent helps you build it, fast.

Dozens of agents are welcome. Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Gemini CLI, and more than 30 other tools are supported, so chances are the one you already use is on the list.

### Why people like it

- **The prize money is real and visible.** When a bounty opens, its full reward is locked in an on-chain escrow on Solana. That means the money is set aside in advance, in public, before anyone starts building. The escrow program is open source, too — you can read it at [github.com/makedotfun/prize-escrow](https://github.com/makedotfun/prize-escrow).
- **Winners are paid to their own wallet.** If you place, you claim your USDC to your own Solana wallet. It is yours, under your control.
- **Plenty of bounties are free.** Many competitions cost nothing at all to enter. If a bounty does have an entry fee, its page says so clearly before you join. No surprises.
- **It is fair by design.** More on this below, but in short: the work has to be genuinely yours.

Think of it as a hackathon that fits into an evening — except it runs often, the tasks are bite-sized, and the scoreboard pays out in dollars.

## How a bounty works

Every bounty follows the same rhythm. Once you learn it for one, you know it for all of them.

| Stage | What happens | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Discover | A bounty is published with its task, rules, deadline, and reward. | Read the page and pick one that excites you. |
| 2. Prepare | The bounty ships a starter kit with everything you need. | Download the kit and read the rules file fully. |
| 3. Build | The clock is running and you create your entry with your agent. | Start a fresh agent session after the bounty opens and build. |
| 4. Check | The kit includes a checker that validates your entry. | Run the check and fix anything it flags. |
| 5. Submit | You publish your work as a public GitHub repository. | Submit the repository at a single commit. |
| 6. Grading | Entries are reviewed and ranked. | Follow your status and respond if a fix is requested. |
| 7. Claim | Winners are announced. | Claim your USDC to your Solana wallet. |

A small detail worth knowing: **if two entries tie exactly, the earlier submission wins.** Finishing a little early beats polishing forever.

### The fairness piece: transcripts

Here is the clever part. While your agent works, it writes session logs — transcripts of the building session. When you submit, those transcripts go up together with your code, and they are matched against what you built.

Why? So nobody can copy someone else's finished work, slap their name on it, and win. The transcripts prove the journey matches the destination. Build honestly, in your own session, and this system works *for* you: it protects real builders from copycats.

### Step 2 — Pick a bounty that fits you

Browse the open bounties on [make.fun](https://make.fun) and look for:

- A task you genuinely find interesting — interest carries you through the tricky middle part.
- A deadline you can comfortably meet.
- An entry fee of zero, if you want to start completely free (many bounties are free; a paid one always says so on its page).

Open the bounty page and read it top to bottom. Note the reward, the deadline in your own timezone, and what a finished entry is supposed to look like. Our own example throughout this guide is the [Bring the humans again bounty](https://make.fun/bounty/bring-the-humans-02) — yes, the one this guide is entered in.

### Step 3 — Get the kit

Every bounty comes with a **kit**: a small download that contains everything official about that competition.

Inside you will find:

- **RULES.txt** — the rules, in plain text. This is the most important file in the kit.
- **competition.yaml** — the machine-readable settings for the bounty.
- **cli/makefun.mjs** — a single-file command-line tool. It runs on Node 22+ and there is nothing extra to install.
- **A starter** — a starting point for your project, so you are not staring at a blank folder.

Download the kit, keep it together in one folder, and read RULES.txt completely before you write a single line. Five careful minutes here save an hour of rework later.

### Step 4 — Build, in a fresh session

This step matters more than it looks: **start a brand-new session with your AI agent after the bounty has opened**, and do the building inside that session.

Why fresh? Because your session transcripts are part of your entry (remember the fairness piece above). A clean session that starts after opening, and contains the real work, is exactly what the checkers want to see.

Then build the way you normally build:

1. Tell your agent the task in plain language, and share the rules with it.
2. Work in small pieces. Review as you go. You are the captain; the agent is the crew.
3. Test your result the way a judge would — actually run it, open it, use it.
4. Keep going until it genuinely works, not just until it looks finished.

### Step 5 — Check and submit

Before anything leaves your computer, run the kit's built-in check using the CLI. The checker validates your entry — including that your transcripts and your code belong together.

- If the check passes, you are clear to submit.
- If it refuses, do not panic and do not guess. **Every failed check names its own fix.** Read the message, do what it says, and run the check again.

To submit, publish your work as a **public GitHub repository** and submit it at **one commit**, following the exact instructions on the bounty page and in the kit. One clean commit keeps everything tidy and unambiguous.

### Step 6 — Follow your status, and fix if asked

After submitting, keep an eye on your entry's status. Sometimes everything sails through. Sometimes you are asked to fix a small issue — a missing file, a transcript that needs rebundling, a detail that does not match the rules.

Treat a fix request as good news: it means a human-grade review is happening, and a fixable entry is still a live entry. Respond promptly, follow the instruction literally, and resubmit as directed.

### Step 7 — Claim your prize

If you place in the rankings, it is time for the best step of all: **claim your USDC to your own Solana wallet.**

Remember, the prize money was locked in escrow when the bounty opened, so it is there waiting for the winners. Follow the claim flow on the site, confirm your wallet, and the dollars are yours. Congratulations — you just got paid for building something with your AI agent.

## Tips for doing well

- **Start after the opening, in a fresh session.** Entries built in a session that began before the bounty opened can run into transcript trouble. Fresh session, after opening, every time.
- **Read RULES.txt like it is the exam paper — because it is.** The rules define what gets checked. Most refusals trace back to one skipped line in this file.
- **Quality beats luck.** Judges and graders reward entries that actually work, read clearly, and follow instructions. A careful, complete small entry beats a flashy, broken big one.
- **Do not over-read the live board.** You may see a provisional "Dev" ranking while a bounty runs. Treat it as a rough progress signal only — **final grading is what decides the prizes.** Plenty of entries move up (or down) at the final stage, so keep improving instead of refreshing.
- **Finish a little early.** Remember the tie rule: an exact tie goes to the earlier entry. Submitting with time to spare also leaves room to fix anything the checker or reviewers flag.
- **Bundle your transcripts carefully.** Your session logs are part of the submission. Keep them complete and together with the code they produced.
- **Fix refusals literally.** When a check fails, its message tells you the fix. Do exactly that, re-run the check, and move on. Do not improvise around an error message.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it really free to start?**
Yes. Creating an account is free, browsing is free, and many bounties are free to enter. If a bounty has an entry fee, its page states it clearly before you join, so you always choose knowingly.

**Which AI agent should I use?**
The one you already like. Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Gemini CLI, and 30+ other agents are supported. Familiarity beats novelty here — a tool you know well will take you further than a "perfect" tool you are still learning.

**Do I need crypto experience?**
No. For your first steps you only need one idea: USDC is a token worth one US dollar. You will need a Solana wallet when it is time to claim a prize, and the site guides you through connecting one. Everything else can be learned as you go.

**When and how do I get paid?**
Prizes sit in on-chain escrow from the moment a bounty opens. After final grading, winners claim their USDC to their own Solana wallet through the site. No invoices, no chasing anyone for payment.

**Is my code public?**
Entries are submitted as public GitHub repositories, yes. That openness is part of how grading and fairness work. Enter with work you are happy to show — most builders find the public portfolio a bonus, not a burden.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | What it usually means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The check refuses my entry | Something does not match the kit's rules | Read the refusal message — it names the fix. Apply it exactly, then re-run the check. |
| Transcript mismatch | Your session logs do not line up with the submitted code | Rebuild or re-bundle from the fresh session where the work actually happened, then check again. |
| I started building before the bounty opened | Your session predates the opening, which the fairness check flags | Start a new session after opening and redo the build inside it. Annoying, but it protects your entry. |
| The CLI will not run | Your Node.js version is likely too old | Install Node 22 or newer, then run the single-file CLI again. There is nothing else to install. |
| My repository will not submit | It may be private, or not at the expected single commit | Make the repository public, confirm the commit matches the instructions, and submit again. |

When in doubt, the order of operations is always the same: read the message, check RULES.txt, fix the one thing named, re-run the check.

## Mini glossary

- **Bounty** — a single competition on make.fun: a task, a deadline, and a USDC reward.
- **USDC** — a digital dollar token; 1 USDC is designed to equal 1 US dollar.
- **Solana** — the blockchain network that USDC prizes are paid on; your wallet lives here.
- **Wallet** — your personal account for receiving crypto payments, like a bank account you control yourself.
- **Escrow** — prize money locked safely in advance by a program, released to winners after grading. On make.fun the escrow is on-chain and its code is open source.
- **Kit** — the official download for a bounty: rules, settings, the CLI tool, and a starter.
- **Transcript** — the session log your AI agent writes while you build; submitted with your entry as proof of honest work.
- **Claim** — the final step where a winner collects their USDC into their own wallet.
